CRS random forest experimental

A random forest of decision trees, each asking yes/no questions about the structure of the models' Canonical Realm Strings: has land ice?, land surface inside the atmosphere?, land ice ↔ ocean coupled? Two models are alike when the trees keep putting them in the same place, and the questions the trees ask show why. See How it works; for the overlap-based view see CRS similarity.

Splits as a decision tree

One summary tree over the real models, built from the forest's likeness: each split is the yes/no question that best divides its models into two internally alike sides. A branch stops once its models are as alike as the slider says (it also sets the Likeness groups). Point at a question or an end group to see its models at the bottom of the screen.

How much the forest relies on each question

Each bar is how much a question helped the forest's trees. A high score means knowing the answer tells you a lot about the rest of a model's structure; a low score means it tells you little. The two examples are worked out from the current forest.

This describes structure as written in the CRS strings, not how well a model performs. With a few dozen models, small differences between bars are noise: compare a few seeds. The tree above chooses its questions differently (the best two-way division of the models), so its first question need not be the top bar. Measure: mean decrease in Gini impurity. Hover a question for other wordings that split the models the same way.

Questions that go together

Pairs of questions whose answers go together across the models, measured by the phi correlation: 1 = always the same answer, 0 = unrelated, −1 = always opposite answers. Pairs about the same realm are left out (they are linked by definition), and so are questions that fewer than 3 models answer yes, or no (one or two models agreeing by chance would look like a perfect match). Counts: both yes · only the first · only the second · neither.
